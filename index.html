Conversion, not reach, is the win

About 2.9% of its viewers subscribed (11/379), against a channel average of about 1.0% (724/69.5k). It is not your most-viewed video (LinkedIn has 547), but it converts about twice as well. Its 2.9% click-through rate is also above the 3.5% channel norm only on volume: 4,479 impressions is the third-highest.

What the audience is telling you

  1. They want a path, not news. The winners are “how to learn / build / get a job with AI”: learning method, job hunt, career shift, no-code, CV, cover letters. Commentary (Elon, “AI isn't a bubble”) gets views but almost no subs.
  2. Long, step-by-step videos (15–25 min) earn subscribers. Shorts do not. Roughly ten videos under a minute have 0 subs in total.
  3. Career and skill-gap audience. Your top three subscriber videos all speak to someone worried about their job or skills.
  4. Mostly new, one-time viewers. 76% are new, only 7% regular. Browse (34%) and external (27%) traffic dominate, so you are being found, not yet kept.
  5. Retention is the weak spot. This video has 10.4 watch hours over 379 views, roughly 1.6 minutes per view on a 17-minute video (about 10%). People subscribe early, then leave.

What to focus on

  1. Make more “learn AI by building” and “AI for your career” videos in this format: named method, steps, live demo.
  2. Fix the first 2 minutes: show the result first, tighten the intro, and put the subscribe ask where drop-off starts.
  3. Stop spending effort on sub-minute Shorts for subscriber growth. Use them only to point to a long video.
  4. Build series around the winners (job hunt, CV, cover letters, career shift) so a new viewer has a next video to watch.
  5. Check each video's audience-retention graph in Studio and cut where it falls.
  6. Next time the metric to watch is subscribers per 100 views, not total views.

Why it plausibly converts viewers into subscribers

1. It names a pain the viewer already feels

The opening (“you watch tutorials, nothing sticks”) hits a universal frustration with AI learning. Viewers self-identify in the first 45 seconds, which is the moment they decide to stay.

2. A named, memorable method

“The Visual Construction Method” gives the idea a label people can repeat. The title promises a secret (“Nobody Teaches”), and the description lists five clear steps: HTML page, record and spot gaps, animate, make interactive, share.

3. Old way vs. new way contrast

Watch → Feel smart → Forget → Repeat against Build → Share → Discover gaps → Master. It's a simple before/after that people remember and share.

4. The method is ongoing, so subscribing is the next step

A weekly MVP and learning “in public” mean one video can't finish the method. The channel is the continuation, which gives a viewer a concrete reason to subscribe.

5. Proof and credibility

200+ GitHub repos, 50+ enterprise contracts, a live AI Security Course and a Codespaces demo. The method is shown on screen, not just described.

6. Free, low-cost and low-risk

The cost breakdown (GitHub Pages free, Codespaces 60 h/month free, under $5/month total vs. $50–200 courses) removes the main objection. “I'm not selling you a course” builds trust.

7. Clear calls to action and a community

“Drop your one AI skill and your repo in the comments”, a free newsletter, templates to fork and a Skool community. Each turns a passive viewer into a participant.

8. Strong structure and discoverability

19 chapters give viewers a map and help search. Tags target “learn AI”, “tutorial hell”, “AI portfolio” and “GitHub Codespaces”, topics with real search demand.

The loop the video teaches

Build a learning page→Record & spot gaps→Animate→Make it interactive→Share & iterate↺

What to repeat in future videos

  1. Open with a pain the viewer feels, in under 45 seconds.
  2. Give the idea a name and a numbered process.
  3. Show a live demo as proof.
  4. Tie “what's next” to the channel (weekly series) so subscribing is the natural step.
  5. Ask one specific question in the comments.
  6. Check YouTube Studio's subscriber-by-video report to validate which of these actually works.
